# Consumer Protection Act, 2019

An Act to provide for protection of the interests of consumers and for the purpose of establishment of consumer disputes redressal commissions and consumer mediation cells. This Act replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 and was notified on 9th August 2019 (Act No. 35 of 2019). Source: [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/).

---

## Section 2: Definitions

**Consumer** (Section 2(7)): Any person who buys any goods or hires or avails any service for a consideration, whether paid or promised, or partly paid and partly promised, or under any system of deferred payment. This includes any user of such goods or beneficiary of such service, other than the person who obtains goods for resale or for any commercial purpose, with the exception of goods bought and used by a person exclusively for earning his livelihood by means of self-employment.

**Complaint** (Section 2(6)): Any allegation in writing made by a complainant regarding an unfair contract or unfair trade practice adopted by any trader or service provider; the goods bought or agreed to be bought suffer from one or more defects; the services hired or availed of suffer from deficiency; the trader or service provider has charged a price in excess of the MRP or the price displayed or agreed upon; or goods or services which are hazardous to life and safety are being offered.

**Deficiency** (Section 2(11)): Any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in the quality, nature, and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law for the time being in force or has been undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract or otherwise in relation to any service. This includes any act of negligence or omission or commission by the service provider causing loss or injury to the consumer.

**Unfair Trade Practice** (Section 2(47)): A trade practice which, for the purpose of promoting the sale, use, or supply of any goods or provision of any service, adopts any unfair method or unfair or deceptive practice. This includes making false or misleading representation about the standard, quality, grade, composition, style, model, or the need for goods or services; falsely representing second-hand goods as new; making false claims about sponsorship, approval, or affiliation; and publishing misleading advertisements.

## Section 9: Establishment of District Commission

The State Government shall establish a District Commission in each district of the State, to be known as the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission. Each District Commission shall consist of a President and not less than two members, one of whom shall be a woman. The District Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed one crore rupees (₹1,00,00,000).

## Section 10: Composition of District Commission

The District Commission shall consist of a President who is or has been or is qualified to be a District Judge, and not less than two members who shall possess a bachelor's degree from a recognized university and shall be not less than 35 years of age. One of the members shall be a woman. The President and members shall have adequate knowledge and experience of at least ten years in dealing with problems relating to economics, law, commerce, accountancy, industry, public affairs, or administration.

## Section 11: Jurisdiction of District Commission

Subject to the other provisions of this Act, the District Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed one crore rupees. A complaint shall be instituted in a District Commission within the jurisdiction of which the opposite party or each of the opposite parties, where there are more than one, at the time of the institution of the complaint, actually and voluntarily resides or carries on business or has a branch office, or personally works for gain.

## Section 12: Manner in Which Complaint Shall Be Made

A complaint in relation to any goods sold or delivered or agreed to be sold or delivered, or any service provided or agreed to be provided, may be filed with the District Commission by the consumer, any recognized consumer association, or the Central or State Government. Every complaint shall be accompanied by such fee and filed in such manner as may be prescribed. The admissibility of the complaint shall be decided within 21 days from the date of filing.

## Section 13: Proceedings Before District Commission

On admission of a complaint, the District Commission shall refer a copy of the admitted complaint to the opposite party directing the opposite party to give its version of the case within a period of 30 days. The opposite party on receipt of the complaint shall give its version to the complaint on plain paper and may also file documents in support of its defence. The District Commission shall, if the complaint so admitted requires the analysis or testing of the goods, refer a sample of the goods for appropriate analysis to the laboratory.

## Section 14: Finding of District Commission

If the District Commission is satisfied that the goods complained against suffer from any of the defects specified in the complaint, or that any of the allegations contained in the complaint about the services are proved, it shall issue an order to the opposite party directing one or more of the following: removal of defect, replacement of goods, return of price, payment of compensation, removal of deficiency, discontinuance of unfair trade practice, or adequate costs to the parties.

## Section 15: Appeal Against Order of District Commission

Any person aggrieved by an order made by the District Commission may prefer an appeal against such order to the State Commission on the grounds of facts or law within a period of 45 days from the date of the order. The State Commission may entertain an appeal after the expiry of the said period if it is satisfied that there was sufficient cause for not filing it within the prescribed period. No appeal shall lie from any order unless the appellant has deposited fifty percent of the amount or twenty-five lakh rupees, whichever is less, as directed by the State Commission.

## Section 16: Constitution of Benches

The President of the District Commission may constitute benches for hearing complaints within the district. Each bench shall consist of not less than two members, one of whom shall be the President. Where the members of a bench differ on any point, the point shall be decided according to the majority opinion, if any; otherwise, the matter shall be referred to the President for hearing on such point.

## Section 17: Transfer of Cases

On the application of the complainant or of its own motion, the State Commission may, at any stage of the proceedings, transfer any complaint pending before one District Commission to another District Commission within the State. Where a complaint has been transferred, the District Commission to which the complaint is transferred may proceed either de novo or from the stage at which it was transferred.

## Section 18: Procedure Applicable to District Commission

The District Commission shall, while trying a complaint, follow such procedure as may be prescribed by the Central Government. The District Commission shall not be bound by the procedure laid down in the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 but shall be guided by the principles of natural justice. The District Commission shall have the powers of a civil court while trying a suit in respect of summoning and enforcing attendance of any defendant or witness, discovery and production of any document, reception of evidence on affidavits, and issuing commissions.

## Section 34: State Commission — Jurisdiction and Powers

The State Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration exceeds one crore rupees (₹1,00,00,000) but does not exceed ten crore rupees (₹10,00,00,000). The State Commission shall also hear appeals against orders of the District Commission within the State. Each State Commission shall consist of a President who is or has been a Judge of a High Court, and not less than four members, one of whom shall be a woman. The State Commission may, if it considers it necessary, transfer any complaint pending before one District Commission to another within the State.

---

## Section 47: National Commission — Jurisdiction and Powers

The National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission shall be established by the Central Government and shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration exceeds ten crore rupees (₹10,00,00,000). The National Commission shall also hear appeals against the orders of any State Commission and entertain matters transferred to it by the Supreme Court. It shall be presided over by a person who is or has been a Judge of the Supreme Court of India. The National Commission shall have the power of judicial review and may call for the records and pass appropriate orders in any consumer dispute pending before or decided by any State Commission where it appears that the State Commission has exercised a jurisdiction not vested in it by law or has failed to exercise a jurisdiction so vested.

---

## Section 35: Filing Procedure for Complaints

A complaint may be filed before the appropriate Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (District, State, or National) having jurisdiction. The complaint can be filed by the consumer to whom the goods are sold or delivered or the service is provided, or by any recognized consumer association whether the consumer is a member of such association or not, or by one or more consumers where there are numerous consumers having the same interest, with the permission of the Commission. The complaint shall contain the name, description, and address of the complainant and opposite party, the facts relating to the complaint, and the relief claimed.

## Section 36: Procedure on Admission of Complaint

The District Commission, State Commission, or National Commission, on receipt of a complaint, shall refer a copy of the complaint to the opposite party directing him to give his version of the case within 30 days. After settlement of the issues, the Commission shall proceed with the hearing of the complaint. Every complaint shall be heard as expeditiously as possible, and endeavour shall be made to decide the complaint within three months from the date of receipt of the notice by the opposite party where the complaint does not require analysis or testing, and within five months if it does.

## Section 37: E-filing and Digital Provisions

Complaints may be filed electronically through the e-Daakhil portal (edaakhil.nic.in), which is the online case filing system for consumer commissions at the National, State, and District levels. The portal enables complainants to file complaints, upload supporting documents, pay fees online, and track the status of their cases without physically visiting the commission. This digitization initiative aims to increase access to consumer justice, particularly for consumers in remote areas. Hearings may also be conducted through video conferencing in accordance with rules prescribed by the Central Government.

## Section 38: Appeal to Supreme Court

Any person aggrieved by an order made by the National Commission may prefer an appeal against such order to the Supreme Court within a period of 30 days from the date of the order. The Supreme Court may entertain the appeal after the expiry of the said period of 30 days if it is satisfied that there was sufficient cause for not filing it within the period. No appeal shall lie from an order passed by the National Commission unless the appellant deposits fifty percent of the amount or fifty lakh rupees, whichever is less.

## Section 69: Limitation Period

The District Commission, the State Commission, or the National Commission shall not admit a complaint unless it is filed within two years from the date on which the cause of action has arisen. Where the complainant satisfies the Commission that he had sufficient cause for not filing the complaint within the prescribed period of two years, the Commission may admit the complaint after the said period. The burden of proving sufficient cause for the delay lies on the complainant. In computing the period of limitation, the time taken by the complainant in pursuing any other remedy before any court, tribunal, or authority shall be excluded.

## Section 84: Application of Product Liability

A product liability action may be brought by a complainant against a product manufacturer, a product service provider, or a product seller, as the case may be, for any harm caused to the complainant on account of a defective product. The product liability action may be brought for any injury to the person, damage to property, or death of a person caused by or resulting from a defective product manufactured, sold, or provided by a product manufacturer, product seller, or product service provider.

## Section 85: Product Manufacturer's Liability

A product manufacturer shall be liable in a product liability action if the product contains a manufacturing defect, is defective in design, or there is a deviation from manufacturing specifications. The manufacturer is also liable if the product does not conform to the express warranty given by the manufacturer, or the product fails to contain adequate instructions for correct use to prevent any harm, or adequate warnings regarding improper or incorrect usage. A product manufacturer shall be liable even if he proves that he was not negligent or fraudulent in making the express warranty of a product.

## Section 86: Product Service Provider's Liability

A product service provider shall be liable in a product liability action if the service was faulty or imperfect, or deficient, or inadequate in quality, nature, or manner of performance. Liability also arises if the service provider acted in a negligent manner, or there was a failure to issue adequate warning or provide accurate instructions regarding the service which could have prevented the harm caused. The service provider shall also be liable if the service did not conform to express warranty or the terms and conditions of the contract.

## Section 87: Product Seller's Liability

A product seller who is not a product manufacturer shall be liable in a product liability action if the product seller has exercised substantial control over the designing, testing, manufacture, packaging, or labelling of a product that caused harm. The seller is also liable if the seller has altered or modified the product, or made an express warranty of the product independent of any express warranty made by the manufacturer, and the product failed to conform to the express warranty so made, causing harm.

## Section 88: Misleading Advertisements — Central Authority Action

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) may, after giving the person an opportunity of being heard, by order, require the withdrawal of the misleading advertisement, or the modification of the advertisement. The CCPA may impose a penalty on a manufacturer or an endorser for a false or misleading advertisement. For the first contravention, a penalty of up to ten lakh rupees (₹10,00,000) may be imposed on the manufacturer, and for every subsequent contravention, up to fifty lakh rupees (₹50,00,000). The manufacturer may also be prohibited from endorsing any product for a period up to one year for the first contravention and up to three years for subsequent contraventions.

## Section 89: Penalties for Misleading Advertisement

Where the CCPA is satisfied that any advertisement is false or misleading and is prejudicial to the interest of any consumer or is in contravention of consumer rights, it may issue directions to the trader or manufacturer or endorser not to continue or repeat such advertisement. If any person fails to comply with the directions of the CCPA, he shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years and with fine which may extend to ten lakh rupees (₹10,00,000). For endorsers, if they have performed due diligence and verified the veracity of claims, they are exempt from liability; however, personal testimony-based endorsements make the endorser equally liable.

## Mediation Provisions (Sections 74-81)

The Act introduces a dedicated chapter on consumer mediation as an alternative dispute resolution mechanism. At every stage of a complaint, the Consumer Commission may direct parties to give their consent for referring the matter to mediation. Consumer mediation cells shall be established and attached to each District Commission, State Commission, and the National Commission. The mediator shall be a person of standing and shall not have any interest, direct or indirect, in the matter under reference. The mediation shall be completed within the period of three months from the date of referral, extendable by a further period of one month with the consent of the parties. No appeal shall lie against the settlement reached through mediation.
