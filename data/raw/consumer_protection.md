# Consumer Protection Act, 2019

**Act No. 35 of 2019** | **Date of Assent: 9th August, 2019**
Source: [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in)

This Act provides for the protection of consumers' interests and for the establishment of authorities for timely and effective administration and settlement of consumer disputes. It repeals and replaces the Consumer Protection Act, 1986.

---

## Section 2: Definitions

**"Consumer"** (Section 2(7)) means any person who buys any goods or hires or avails any service for a consideration which has been paid or promised or partly paid and partly promised, or under any system of deferred payment. It includes any user of such goods or beneficiary of such services when use is made with the approval of the buyer, but does not include a person who obtains goods for resale or for any commercial purpose. A person who obtains goods for earning a livelihood by means of self-employment is not excluded from this definition.

**"Complaint"** (Section 2(6)) means any allegation in writing made by a complainant relating to one or more of the following: an unfair trade practice or restrictive trade practice adopted by a trader or service provider; goods bought or agreed to be bought suffering from one or more defects; services hired or availed of suffering from deficiency; a trader or service provider charging a price in excess of the price displayed, affixed, or agreed upon; goods or services that are hazardous to life and safety; or unfair contracts.

**"Deficiency"** (Section 2(11)) means any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in the quality, nature, and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law for the time being in force or has been undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract or otherwise in relation to any service. The definition also covers any act of negligence or omission or commission by a service provider which causes loss or injury to the consumer.

**"Unfair Trade Practice"** (Section 2(47)) means a trade practice which, for the purpose of promoting the sale, use, or supply of any goods or for the provision of any service, adopts any unfair method or unfair or deceptive practice. This includes making a false or misleading representation about the standard, quality, grade, composition, style, or model of goods or services; permitting the publication of any advertisement whether in any newspaper or otherwise, for the sale or supply at a bargain price of goods or services that are not intended to be offered for sale at the bargain price; and making false or misleading representation concerning the need for or the usefulness of goods or services.

**"Defect"** (Section 2(10)) means any fault, imperfection, or shortcoming in the quality, quantity, potency, purity, or standard which is required to be maintained by or under any law for the time being in force or under any contract, express or implied, or as is claimed by the trader in any manner whatsoever in relation to any goods.

## Section 9: Establishment of District Commission

The State Government shall, by notification, establish a District Commission in each district of the State, which may be known by such name as specified in the notification. The District Commission shall be headed by a President who is or has been or is qualified to be a District Judge, along with not less than two members, of whom at least one shall be a woman. The District Commission shall ordinarily function in the district headquarters and may also hold circuit benches in other places within the district as the State Government may prescribe.

## Section 10: Composition of District Commission

Each District Commission shall consist of a President and not less than two and not more than such number of members as may be prescribed by the State Government, in consultation with the Central Government. Every appointment under this section shall be made by the State Government on the recommendation of a Selection Committee consisting of the President of the State Commission (Chairperson), the Secretary of the State Law Department, and the Secretary of the Department dealing with consumer affairs.

## Section 11: Jurisdiction of District Commission

The District Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed one crore rupees (₹1,00,00,000). A complaint shall be instituted in a District Commission within the local limits of whose jurisdiction the opposite party or each of the opposite parties actually and voluntarily resides or carries on business or has a branch office or personally works for gain at the time of institution of the complaint. The territorial jurisdiction also extends to the area where the cause of action, wholly or in part, arises.

## Section 12: Manner in Which Complaint Shall Be Made

A complaint may be filed in the District Commission by the consumer to whom the goods are sold or delivered or agreed to be sold or delivered, or the service is provided or agreed to be provided. Additionally, a complaint may be filed by any recognised consumer association, whether the consumer is a member or not; one or more consumers where there are numerous consumers having the same interest, with the permission of the District Commission, on behalf of or for the benefit of all consumers so interested; or the Central Government, the Central Authority, or the State Government, as the case may be. Every complaint shall be accompanied by such amount of fee and payable in such manner as may be prescribed.

## Section 13: Procedure on Admission of Complaint

The District Commission shall, on receipt of a complaint, refer the complaint to the opposite party within twenty-one days from the date of its admission, directing the opposite party to give a version of his case within thirty days of receipt. Where the opposite party denies or disputes the allegations or fails to present their case within thirty days, the District Commission shall settle the dispute on the basis of evidence brought before it. If the complaint involves analysis or testing of goods, the District Commission shall obtain a sample of the goods and refer it to an appropriate laboratory for analysis or testing.

## Section 14: Reference to Mediation

At any stage of the proceedings or prior to taking evidence, the District Commission may, with the consent of both parties, refer the matter for mediation under Chapter V. The mediation shall be completed within forty-five days from the date of such reference, which may be extended for a further fifteen days with the consent of both parties. Where no settlement is reached through mediation, the District Commission shall continue to hear the complaint from the stage at which the complaint was referred for mediation.

## Section 15: Powers of the District Commission

The District Commission shall have the powers of a civil court while trying a suit under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 in respect of summoning and enforcing the attendance of any defendant or witness; the discovery and production of any document or other material object producible as evidence; the reception of evidence on affidavits; the requisitioning of the report of the concerned analysis or testing from the appropriate laboratory; issuing commissions for the examination of witnesses; and any other matter which may be prescribed.

## Section 16-17: Dismissal and Appeal

The District Commission shall have the power to dismiss complaints that are frivolous or vexatious, and may impose costs on the complainant for such filing. Any person aggrieved by an order made by the District Commission may prefer an appeal against such order to the State Commission on the grounds of facts or law within a period of forty-five days from the date of the order, in such form and manner as may be prescribed.

## Section 18: Relief Available to Consumers

Where the District Commission is satisfied that the goods complained against suffer from any of the defects specified in the complaint or that any of the allegations in the complaint about the services are proved, the Commission may issue orders directing one or more of the following: removal of defects from the goods; replacement of the goods with new goods of similar description; return to the complainant the price paid; payment of compensation to the consumer for any loss or injury suffered; discontinuance of unfair or restrictive trade practice and not to repeat it; withdrawal of hazardous goods from sale; refund of charges paid; adequate costs to parties; or any other order as it deems fit.

## Section 34: State Commission — Jurisdiction

The State Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of goods or services paid as consideration exceeds one crore rupees (₹1,00,00,000) but does not exceed ten crore rupees (₹10,00,00,000). It shall also act as an appellate authority against orders of any District Commission within the State. The State Commission may also call for records and pass appropriate orders in any consumer dispute which is pending before or has been decided by any District Commission, where it appears that the District Commission has exercised jurisdiction not vested in it by law, or has failed to exercise jurisdiction so vested, or has acted in exercise of its jurisdiction illegally or with material irregularity. The State Commission shall consist of a President who is or has been a Judge of a High Court and not less than four members, one of whom shall be a woman.

---

## Section 47: National Commission — Jurisdiction

The National Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of goods or services paid as consideration exceeds ten crore rupees (₹10,00,00,000). It is also the appellate authority against orders passed by any State Commission and may call for the records and pass appropriate orders in any consumer dispute pending before or decided by any State Commission. The National Commission shall consist of a President who is or has been a Judge of the Supreme Court and not less than four members, one of whom shall be a woman. It is established by the Central Government by notification and has its principal bench at New Delhi with the power to set up circuit benches in other locations.

---

## Section 35: Filing of Complaint

A complaint shall be filed with the appropriate Consumer Commission (District, State, or National) based on the pecuniary jurisdiction and territorial jurisdiction. Every complaint shall be in writing and may be filed electronically through the e-Daakhil portal (https://edaakhil.nic.in), which is the official electronic filing system for consumer cases. The complaint must contain the name, description, and address of the complainant and opposite party; the facts relating to the complaint and when and where it arose; documents in support of the allegations; and the relief which the complainant claims. The complaint shall be accompanied by the prescribed fee.

## Section 36: Procedure on Receipt of Complaint

On receiving a complaint, the Consumer Commission shall determine if the complaint is admissible and whether it falls within its jurisdiction. The Commission shall admit the complaint and cause a copy of the complaint to be served on the opposite party within twenty-one days from the date of admission, directing the opposite party to file a written response within thirty days. If the Commission is satisfied that the complaint does not require further inquiry, it may reject the complaint with reasons.

## Section 37: Appeal Against Orders of District Commission

Any person aggrieved by an order made by the District Commission may prefer an appeal to the State Commission within forty-five days from the date of the order. The State Commission may entertain an appeal after the expiry of the period of forty-five days if it is satisfied that there was sufficient cause for not filing it within that period. No appeal by a person who is required to pay any amount in terms of an order of the District Commission shall be entertained by the State Commission unless the appellant has deposited in the prescribed manner fifty percent of the amount or twenty-five lakh rupees, whichever is less.

## Section 38: Appeal Against Orders of State Commission

Any person aggrieved by an order made by the State Commission may prefer an appeal to the National Commission within forty-five days from the date of the order. The National Commission may entertain an appeal after the expiry of the period of forty-five days if it is satisfied that there was sufficient cause for not filing it within that period. No appeal by a person who is required to pay any amount in terms of an order of the State Commission shall be entertained by the National Commission unless the appellant has deposited in the prescribed manner fifty percent of the amount or fifty lakh rupees, whichever is less.

---

## Section 39: Admissibility of Complaint

The Consumer Commission shall not admit a complaint unless it is filed within the period of two years from the date on which the cause of action has arisen. A complaint may be entertained after the period prescribed if the complainant satisfies the Commission that they had sufficient cause for not filing the complaint within such period. If the Commission finds that the complaint is barred by time, it shall record the reasons for such conclusion and reject the complaint. Where the complaint is related to goods, the Commission shall ascertain whether the goods comply with the standards mandated by any law.

---

## Section 69: Limitation Period

The Consumer Commission shall not admit a complaint unless it is filed within the period of limitation of two years from the date on which the cause of action has arisen. However, a complaint may be entertained after the period of two years if the complainant satisfies the Commission that he had sufficient cause for not filing the complaint within such period, and the delay has been condoned by recording reasons in writing. The limitation period is counted from the date the defect or deficiency was first noticed or should have been reasonably noticed by the consumer. In cases involving repeated deficiency, the limitation period runs afresh from each instance.

## Section 84: Product Liability

A product liability action may be brought by a complainant against a product manufacturer, product service provider, or product seller, as the case may be, for any harm caused to the complainant by a defective product manufactured by the product manufacturer or serviced by the product service provider or sold by the product seller. The complainant must establish, on a balance of probabilities, the defect in the product, the harm caused, and the causal link between the defect and the harm. A product liability action may be brought even without proving privity of contract between the complainant and the product manufacturer, product service provider, or product seller.

## Section 85: Liability of Product Manufacturer

A product manufacturer shall be liable in a product liability action if the product contains a manufacturing defect, or is defective in design, or there is a deviation from manufacturing specifications, or the product does not conform to the express warranty, or the product fails to contain adequate instructions of correct usage to prevent any harm or any warning regarding improper or incorrect usage. The manufacturer shall not be liable if they can prove that the product was misused, altered, or modified by the consumer and such misuse was not reasonably foreseeable. Manufacturing defect means a deviation from the design specifications, formulae, or performance standards of the product.

## Section 86: Liability of Product Service Provider

A product service provider shall be liable in a product liability action if the service was faulty or imperfect or deficient or inadequate in quality, nature, or manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law or has been undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract. The product service provider shall also be liable when they have made an express warranty or had acted in a negligent manner which has resulted in harm. The service provider is required to warn consumers about any risks associated with the service and must adopt adequate safety measures.

## Section 87: Liability of Product Seller

A product seller who is not a product manufacturer shall be liable in a product liability action if the product seller has exercised substantial control over the designing, testing, manufacture, packaging, or labelling of a product that caused harm. The seller shall also be liable if they have altered or modified the product and such alteration or modification was the substantial factor in causing the harm, or if the seller has made an express warranty of the product independent of any express warranty made by the manufacturer. Additionally, a product seller shall be liable where the identity of the product manufacturer is not known or where they have failed to exercise reasonable care in assembling, inspecting, or maintaining the product.

## Section 88: Punishment for Misleading Advertisement

Any manufacturer or service provider who causes a false or misleading advertisement to be made which is prejudicial to the interest of consumers shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years and with fine which may extend to ten lakh rupees (₹10,00,000). For every subsequent offence, the term of imprisonment may extend to five years and the fine may extend to fifty lakh rupees (₹50,00,000). The Central Authority (CCPA) may, by order, require the concerned trader, manufacturer, endorser, advertiser, or publisher to discontinue or modify the advertisement, and impose a penalty up to ten lakh rupees on the manufacturer or endorser and up to fifty lakh rupees for subsequent contravention.

## Section 89: Punishment for Manufacturing, Selling, Distributing, or Importing Adulterated/Spurious Products

Any person who manufactures, sells, stores, distributes, or imports any product containing an adulterant shall be punished, if such act does not result in any injury to the consumer, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to six months and with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees (₹1,00,000). If the adulterated product results in injury not amounting to grievous hurt, the imprisonment may extend to one year and the fine to three lakh rupees. If it results in grievous hurt, the imprisonment shall be for a term not less than seven years and the fine shall not be less than five lakh rupees. If the injury results in death, the punishment shall be not less than seven years imprisonment extendable to imprisonment for life and a fine not less than ten lakh rupees.

## Mediation Provisions (Chapter V — Sections 74-81)

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 introduced an alternative dispute resolution mechanism through mediation. At any stage of the complaint, the Consumer Commission may, with the consent of the parties, refer the matter for mediation to the Consumer Mediation Cell attached to each Consumer Commission. The mediation shall be conducted by a mediator chosen from a panel of mediators maintained by the Consumer Mediation Cell. The mediation process must be completed within forty-five days from the date of referral, which period may be extended for a further fifteen days with the consent of both parties.

The mediator shall facilitate voluntary resolution of the dispute and shall not impose any terms of settlement on the parties. If the mediation is successful and a settlement is reached, the terms of settlement are reduced to writing, signed by both parties, and submitted to the Consumer Commission which shall pass an order recording the settlement. Such a settlement has the effect of a decree or order of the Commission and is binding on the parties. If no settlement is reached, the Commission shall continue hearing the complaint from the stage at which it was referred for mediation. The mediation proceedings are confidential and communications made during mediation are not admissible as evidence in any subsequent proceedings.

---

## Key Remedies and Enforcement

Consumer Commissions may order: refund of the price paid; removal of defects; replacement of goods; compensation for loss or injury; punitive damages in cases of negligence; discontinuance of unfair trade practices; payment of adequate costs; or any combination of these reliefs. Orders of the Consumer Commission are executable as a decree of a civil court. Non-compliance with orders of the Commission may lead to attachment and sale of the property of the person not complying, or detention in civil prison for a term not less than one month but which may extend to three years, or both.
