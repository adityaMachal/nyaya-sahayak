# Right to Information Act, 2005

**Act No. 22 of 2005** | **Date of Assent: 15th June, 2005**
Source: [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in)

An Act to provide for setting out the practical regime of right to information for citizens to secure access to information under the control of public authorities, in order to promote transparency and accountability in the working of every public authority. This Act applies to the whole of India except the State of Jammu and Kashmir (which had its own J&K RTI Act, 2009, until 2019 when the Central Act became applicable).

---

## Section 2: Definitions

**"Information"** (Section 2(f)) means any material in any form, including records, documents, memos, e-mails, opinions, advices, press releases, circulars, orders, logbooks, contracts, reports, papers, samples, models, data material held in any electronic form. It also includes information relating to any private body which can be accessed by a public authority under any other law for the time being in force. This is an exhaustive definition that covers virtually all forms of recorded knowledge held by or accessible to public authorities.

**"Public Authority"** (Section 2(h)) means any authority or body or institution of self-government established or constituted by or under the Constitution; by any other law made by Parliament; by any other law made by State Legislature; by notification issued or order made by the appropriate Government. This includes bodies owned, controlled, or substantially financed by the Government, as well as non-Government organisations substantially financed, directly or indirectly, by funds provided by the appropriate Government.

**"Right to Information"** (Section 2(j)) means the right to information accessible under this Act which is held by or under the control of any public authority and includes the right to inspection of work, documents, records; taking notes, extracts, or certified copies of documents or records; taking certified samples of material; and obtaining information in the form of diskettes, floppies, tapes, video cassettes, or in any other electronic mode or through printouts where such information is stored in a computer or in any other device.

**"Record"** (Section 2(i)) includes any document, manuscript, and file; any microfilm, microfiche, and facsimile copy of a document; any reproduction of image or images embodied in such microfilm; and any other material produced by a computer or any other device.

**"Public Information Officer" (PIO)** (Section 2(c)(ii) read with Section 5) means an officer designated by the public authority to provide information to persons requesting for the same under this Act. Every public authority is required to designate as many officers as Central/State Public Information Officers in all administrative units or offices as may be necessary.

---

## Section 3: Right to Information

Subject to the provisions of this Act, all citizens shall have the right to information. This is the foundational provision of the Act that establishes the statutory right of every citizen of India to access information held by or under the control of any public authority. The right is not absolute and is subject to the exemptions specified in Section 8 and Section 9 of the Act. Non-citizens (foreign nationals) do not have a right to file RTI applications under this Act.

---

## Section 4: Obligations of Public Authorities (Proactive Disclosure)

Every public authority shall maintain all its records duly catalogued and indexed in a manner and form which facilitates the right to information, and ensure that all records that are appropriate to be computerised are computerised within a reasonable time and connected through a network all over the country. Every public authority shall publish within one hundred and twenty days of the enactment:

- The particulars of its organisation, functions, and duties;
- The powers and duties of its officers and employees;
- The procedure followed in the decision-making process, including channels of supervision and accountability;
- The norms set for discharge of its functions;
- The rules, regulations, instructions, manuals, and records held by it or under its control;
- A statement of categories of documents that are held by it or under its control;
- The particulars of any arrangement for consultation with or representation by members of the public;
- A directory of its officers and employees;
- The monthly remuneration received by each officer and employee, including the system of compensation;
- The budget allocated to each of its agency, indicating the particulars of all plans, proposed expenditures, and reports on disbursements made;
- The manner of execution of subsidy programmes, including the amounts allocated and details of beneficiaries; and
- Particulars of recipients of concessions, permits, or authorisations granted.

It shall be a constant endeavour of every public authority to take steps in accordance with the requirements to provide as much information suo motu to the public at regular intervals through various means of communications, including the internet, so that the public have minimum resort to the use of this Act.

---

## Section 5: Designation of Public Information Officers (PIO)

Every public authority shall, within one hundred days of the enactment of this Act, designate as many officers as the Central Public Information Officers or State Public Information Officers, as the case may be, in all administrative units or offices under it as may be necessary to provide information to persons requesting for the information under this Act. Every public authority shall also designate an officer at each sub-divisional or sub-district level as a Central Assistant Public Information Officer or State Assistant Public Information Officer (APIO), who shall receive the applications for information or appeals and forward the same to the Central or State PIO or the Appellate Authority within five days.

The PIO shall deal with requests from persons seeking information and render reasonable assistance to the persons seeking such information. Where a request is received by the PIO for information which is held by another public authority or the subject matter of which is more closely connected with the functions of another public authority, the PIO shall transfer the request to that other public authority within five days and inform the applicant about such transfer.

---

## Section 6: How to File an RTI Request

A person who desires to obtain any information under this Act shall make a request in writing or through electronic means in English or Hindi or in the official language of the area in which the application is being made. The request shall be accompanied by an application fee of **₹10 (ten rupees)** as may be prescribed by the appropriate Government. No fee is required to be paid by persons who are below the poverty line as may be determined by the appropriate Government.

The applicant is not required to give any reason for requesting the information or any other personal details except those that may be necessary for contacting the applicant. The request should be addressed to the Central/State Public Information Officer of the concerned public authority. The applicant must provide sufficient details to identify the information sought but is not required to specify under which provision of law the information is being sought.

**Modes of filing:**
- In person at the office of the public authority
- By post/registered post to the PIO
- **Online via https://rtionline.gov.in** (for Central Government departments and ministries)
- Through the APIO at the sub-divisional level who will forward it within 5 days

**Fee structure:** Beyond the application fee of ₹10, additional fees may be charged for providing information, at rates prescribed by the Government — typically ₹2 per page of A4 size paper (created or copied); actual charge or cost price for larger documents; ₹50 per diskette or floppy; and the actual cost for samples or models. If the applicant belongs to the Below Poverty Line (BPL) category, no fee is chargeable, but the applicant must furnish a BPL certificate.

---

## Section 7: Response Timeline — Disposal of Request

Subject to the proviso to sub-section (2) of Section 5 or the proviso to sub-section (3) of Section 6, the Central/State Public Information Officer on receipt of a request shall, as expeditiously as possible, and in any case within **thirty days** of the receipt of the request, either provide the information on payment of such fee as may be prescribed or reject the request for any of the reasons specified in Sections 8 and 9.

Where the information sought concerns the **life or liberty** of a person, the same shall be provided within **forty-eight hours** of the receipt of the request. If the PIO fails to give a decision on the request within the prescribed period, the PIO shall be deemed to have refused the request.

Where a request has been rejected, the PIO shall communicate to the requester: the reasons for such rejection; the period within which an appeal against such rejection may be preferred; and the particulars of the Appellate Authority. Where the information is provided in a form other than the one requested, the PIO shall give the reasons for providing it in that form. The PIO shall provide information in the form in which it is requested unless it would disproportionately divert the resources of the public authority.

---

## Section 8: Exemptions from Disclosure

Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, there shall be no obligation to give any citizen information which would prejudicially affect:

1. **Sovereignty and integrity of India**, the security, strategic, scientific, or economic interests of the State, relations with foreign States, or lead to incitement of an offence;
2. Information which has been **expressly forbidden** to be published by any court of law or tribunal, or the disclosure of which may constitute contempt of court;
3. Information the disclosure of which would cause a **breach of privilege** of Parliament or the State Legislature;
4. Information including **commercial confidence, trade secrets**, or intellectual property, the disclosure of which would harm the competitive position of a third party, unless the competent authority is satisfied that larger public interest warrants the disclosure;
5. Information available to a person in his **fiduciary relationship**, unless the competent authority is satisfied that the larger public interest warrants the disclosure;
6. Information received in confidence from a **foreign Government**;
7. Information the disclosure of which would **endanger the life or physical safety** of any person or identify the source of information given in confidence for law enforcement or security purposes;
8. Information which would impede the **process of investigation** or apprehension or prosecution of offenders;
9. **Cabinet papers** including records of deliberations of the Council of Ministers, Secretaries, and other officers — provided that after the decision has been taken and the matter is complete, the reasons for the decision shall be made public;
10. Information which relates to **personal information** the disclosure of which has no relationship to any public activity or interest, or which would cause unwarranted invasion of the privacy of the individual — unless the PIO or appellate authority is satisfied that larger public interest justifies disclosure.

**Important proviso:** Information which cannot be denied to Parliament or a State Legislature shall not be denied to any person. Notwithstanding exemptions, a public authority may allow access to information if the **public interest in disclosure outweighs the harm** to the protected interests (Section 8(2)). Additionally, information relating to allegations of corruption and human rights violations cannot be withheld, subject to approval of the Information Commission (Section 8(2) read with Section 24).

---

## Section 11: Third Party Information

Where a PIO intends to disclose any information or record, or part thereof, on a request made under this Act, which relates to or has been supplied by a third party and has been treated as confidential by that third party, the PIO shall within five days from the receipt of the request give a written notice to such third party of the request and of the fact that the PIO intends to disclose the information. The third party shall be given an opportunity to make a representation against the proposed disclosure within ten days from the date of receipt of such notice.

The PIO shall, within forty days from receipt of the request, make a decision as to whether or not to disclose the information, taking into consideration the representation made by the third party. The third party may file an appeal to the Appellate Authority and the Information Commission against the decision of the PIO. The third party must be informed of their right to appeal and the relevant timelines for doing so.

---

## Section 18: Powers and Functions of the Information Commission

The Central Information Commission or State Information Commission, as the case may be, shall receive and inquire into a complaint from any person who has been unable to submit a request to a PIO because no such officer has been appointed, or who has been refused access to information, or who has not been given a response within the time specified, or who has been required to pay an unreasonable fee, or who believes that the information given is incomplete, misleading, or false. The Commission may also inquire into any matter on its own motion. During inquiry, the Commission has the same powers as are vested in a civil court, including summoning and enforcing attendance of persons, compelling production of documents, and receiving evidence on affidavit.

## Section 19: First Appeal and Second Appeal

**First Appeal (Section 19(1)):** Any person who does not receive a decision within the time specified in Section 7, or is aggrieved by a decision of the PIO, may within **thirty days** from the expiry of such period or from the receipt of such a decision, prefer an appeal to such officer who is senior in rank to the PIO in each public authority, designated as the **First Appellate Authority**. The First Appellate Authority shall dispose of the appeal within thirty days of the receipt of the appeal, extendable to a total of forty-five days with recorded reasons.

**Second Appeal (Section 19(3)):** A second appeal against the decision of the First Appellate Authority shall lie within **ninety days** from the date on which the decision should have been made or was actually received, with the **Central Information Commission** (for Central Government matters) or the **State Information Commission** (for State Government matters). The burden of proving that a denial of a request was justified shall be on the PIO who denied the request. The Information Commission may, while deciding any appeal, require the public authority to take necessary steps including providing access to information, appointing PIOs, publishing certain categories of information, making necessary changes to practices relating to management of records, and providing training on the right to information.

---

## Section 20: Penalties for Non-Compliance

Where the Central/State Information Commission at the time of deciding any complaint or appeal is of the opinion that the PIO has, without any reasonable cause, refused to receive an application for information or has not furnished information within the time specified or malafidely denied the request for information or knowingly given incorrect, incomplete, or misleading information or destroyed information which was the subject of the request or obstructed the furnishing of information, it shall impose a **penalty of two hundred and fifty rupees (₹250) each day** till the application is received or information is furnished, provided that the total amount of such penalty shall not exceed **twenty-five thousand rupees (₹25,000)**.

The Information Commission shall, before imposing any penalty, give the PIO a reasonable opportunity of being heard. The Commission may also recommend disciplinary action against the PIO under the service rules applicable to them. Where the PIO has acted reasonably and diligently, no penalty shall be imposed. The penalty is imposed on the PIO personally and not on the public authority. Additionally, the Commission may order compensation to be paid to the complainant for any loss or other detriment suffered.

## Online RTI Filing — rtionline.gov.in

The Government of India has established an online portal at **https://rtionline.gov.in** for filing RTI applications electronically to Central Government ministries and departments. The portal allows citizens to:

- File RTI applications online by paying the ₹10 fee through internet banking, credit/debit card, or UPI;
- File first appeals online to the First Appellate Authority;
- Track the status of filed RTI applications;
- View the response received from the public authority.

The portal is available 24/7 and eliminates the need for physical visits or postal correspondence. Applications filed online are deemed to have been received by the PIO on the date of successful submission. For State Government departments and agencies, many states have their own RTI portals, such as Maharashtra's RTI Online portal. BPL applicants may still need to apply through offline modes to avoid the fee, as the online portal requires fee payment at the time of filing.
