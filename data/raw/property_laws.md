# Property Laws of India

Covers the Transfer of Property Act 1882 (TPA), Registration Act 1908, Tenancy and Rent Control laws, and the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016 (RERA).

Source: India Code (https://www.indiacode.nic.in)

# Transfer of Property Act, 1882

The Transfer of Property Act, 1882 governs the transfer of property between living persons (inter vivos transfers) in India. It defines and regulates the various modes of transferring property including sale, mortgage, lease, exchange, and gift. The Act applies to the whole of India except the State of Jammu & Kashmir (now reorganized) and certain areas specified therein.

## Section 5: Transfer of Property Defined

"Transfer of property" means an act by which a living person conveys property, in present or in future, to one or more other living persons, or to himself, or to himself and one or more other living persons. The term "living person" includes a company or association or body of individuals, whether incorporated or not. The transfer may be made in the manner and to the extent permitted by the Act. A transfer of property passes forthwith to the transferee all the interest which the transferor is then capable of passing in the property, unless a different intention is expressed or necessarily implied.

## Section 54: Sale of Immovable Property

"Sale" is a transfer of ownership in exchange for a price paid or promised or part-paid and part-promised. A sale of tangible immovable property of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards, or in the case of a reversion or other intangible thing, can be made only by a registered instrument. For tangible immovable property of a value less than one hundred rupees, the transfer may be made either by a registered instrument or by delivery of the property. The contract for sale of immovable property is a contract that a sale of such property shall take place on terms settled between the parties. A contract for sale does not, of itself, create any interest in or charge on such property — it merely creates a right to obtain the sale in the purchaser's favour.

## Section 58: Mortgage of Immovable Property — Definition

A mortgage is the transfer of an interest in specific immovable property for the purpose of securing the payment of money advanced or to be advanced by way of loan, an existing or future debt, or the performance of an engagement which may give rise to a pecuniary liability. The transferor is called a mortgagor, the transferee a mortgagee, the principal money and interest the mortgage-money, and the instrument (if any) by which the transfer is effected is called a mortgage-deed.

## Sections 58-67: Types of Mortgages

**Simple Mortgage (Section 58(b)):** Where, without delivering possession of the mortgaged property, the mortgagor binds himself personally to pay the mortgage-money, and agrees that in the event of his failing to pay, the mortgagee shall have a right to cause the mortgaged property to be sold and the proceeds of sale to be applied in payment of the mortgage-money. The mortgagee does not get possession of the property.

**Usufructuary Mortgage (Section 58(d)):** Where the mortgagor delivers possession of the mortgaged property to the mortgagee and authorizes him to retain such possession until payment of the mortgage-money, and to receive the rents and profits accruing from the property and appropriate them in lieu of interest or in payment of the mortgage-money, or both. The mortgagee cannot sue for the mortgage-money — recovery is only through the usufruct.

**English Mortgage (Section 58(e)):** Where the mortgagor binds himself to repay the mortgage-money on a certain date, and transfers the mortgaged property absolutely to the mortgagee, but subject to a proviso that he will re-transfer the property to the mortgagor upon payment of the mortgage-money as agreed. The mortgagee has absolute ownership until repayment.

**Equitable Mortgage / Mortgage by Deposit of Title Deeds (Section 58(f)):** Where a person in any of the specified towns (or towns notified by the State Government) delivers to a creditor or his agent documents of title to immovable property, with intent to create a security thereon, the transaction is called a mortgage by deposit of title deeds. No formal mortgage deed is required; the deposit itself creates the mortgage.

**Anomalous Mortgage (Section 58(g)):** A mortgage which is not a simple mortgage, a mortgage by conditional sale, a usufructuary mortgage, an English mortgage, or a mortgage by deposit of title deeds is called an anomalous mortgage. Such a mortgage may combine features of two or more types of mortgages.

## Section 60: Right of Mortgagor to Redeem

At any time after the principal money has become due, the mortgagor has a right, on payment or tender of the mortgage-money, to require the mortgagee to deliver to him the mortgage-deed and all documents relating to the mortgaged property, to deliver possession of the property, and to re-transfer the property at the cost of the mortgagor. This right is called the right to redeem and it cannot be fettered or clogged.

## Section 67: Right to Foreclose or Sell

In the absence of a contract to the contrary, the mortgagee has, at any time after the mortgage-money has become due and before the mortgage is redeemed, a right to obtain an order that the mortgagor shall be debarred from exercising his right of redemption (foreclosure) or a right to obtain an order for sale of the mortgaged property.

## Section 105: Lease — Definition

A lease of immovable property is a transfer of a right to enjoy such property, made for a certain time, express or implied, or in perpetuity, in consideration of a price paid or promised, or of money, a share of crops, service, or any other thing of value, to be rendered periodically or on specified occasions to the transferor by the transferee, who accepts the transfer on such terms. The transferor is called the lessor, the transferee is called the lessee, the price is called the premium, and the money, share, service, or other thing to be so rendered is called the rent.

## Section 106: Duration of Certain Leases and Notice of Termination

In the absence of a contract or local usage to the contrary: (a) a lease of immovable property for agricultural or manufacturing purposes shall be deemed to be a lease from year to year, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by six months' notice; (b) a lease of immovable property for any other purpose shall be deemed to be a lease from month to month, terminable by fifteen days' notice by either party. The notice must expire with the end of a month of the tenancy. The notice must be in writing and must be served in the manner prescribed.

## Section 108: Rights and Liabilities of Lessor and Lessee

**Lessor's Rights and Liabilities:** The lessor is bound to disclose to the lessee any material defect in the property which the lessor is aware of and which the lessee could not discover with ordinary care. The lessor shall put the lessee in possession of the property. The lessor shall be deemed to contract with the lessee that if the lessee pays the rent and performs the conditions of the lease, the lessee may hold the property during the time limited by the lease without interruption (covenant of quiet enjoyment).

**Lessee's Rights and Liabilities:** The lessee is entitled to the benefits of accession to the property and may use the property and its products. The lessee may remove, at any time during the continuance of the lease, all things attached by him to the property (right to fixtures), provided he leaves the property in the state in which he received it. The lessee is bound to disclose to the lessor any fact regarding the property which increases its value, to pay the rent, to maintain the property in as good condition as it was in when received (reasonable wear and tear excepted), and not to use the property for purposes other than those for which it was leased.

## Section 111: Determination of Lease

A lease of immovable property determines (terminates): (a) by efflux of time limited thereby; (b) where such time is limited conditionally, on the happening of the event; (c) where the interest of the lessor in the property terminates on an event which the lessor had no power to prevent; (d) in case the interests of the lessee and lessor in the property become vested in the same person (merger); (e) by express surrender; (f) by implied surrender; (g) by forfeiture — the lessee breaks an express condition, or renounces his character as such, or acts in a manner contrary to the terms of the lease; (h) on the expiration of a notice to determine the lease as provided in Section 106.

# Registration Act, 1908

## Key Provisions of the Registration Act

The Registration Act, 1908 mandates compulsory registration of certain documents to ensure their legal validity and evidentiary value. The primary purpose of registration is to provide publicity, prevent fraud, and ensure that a person dealing with property may ascertain whether any prior interest in the property has been created.

**Compulsory Registration (Section 17):** Documents including instruments of gift of immovable property, non-testamentary instruments transferring or creating any interest in immovable property of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards, and leases of immovable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent must be registered. Practically, leases for a period exceeding 11 months require mandatory registration. Unregistered documents that are required to be registered are inadmissible in evidence and do not create any right or interest in the property.

**Time for Registration (Section 23):** Documents must be presented for registration within four months from the date of execution. If not presented within this period, registration may be allowed with a late fee within a further period of four months.

# Tenant Rights and Rent Control

## General Principles of Tenant Protection

Tenancy law in India is primarily state-specific, with each state having its own Rent Control Act or Rent Regulation Act. However, certain general principles are common across most states. Tenants enjoy protection against arbitrary eviction — a landlord cannot evict a tenant except through due process of law and on grounds recognized by the applicable Rent Control Act. Tenants have the right to essential services such as water, electricity, and sanitation, and the landlord cannot cut off these services to force eviction.

**Right to Receipt:** A tenant is entitled to a receipt for rent paid to the landlord. This receipt serves as evidence of payment and protects the tenant against claims of non-payment. If a landlord refuses to accept rent, the tenant may deposit the rent in court to avoid being considered a defaulter.

## Grounds for Eviction

The grounds for eviction are generally codified under state Rent Control Acts and typically include:

1. **Non-Payment of Rent:** Where the tenant has defaulted in payment of rent for a specified period (usually two or more months). However, the tenant is usually given an opportunity to pay arrears before an eviction order is passed.
2. **Subletting Without Permission:** Where the tenant has sublet the whole or part of the premises without the written consent of the landlord, the landlord may seek eviction.
3. **Bona Fide Personal Need:** Where the landlord requires the premises for his own use or for the use of any member of his family, and the landlord has no other reasonably suitable accommodation. The court must be satisfied of the genuineness of the need.
4. **Structural Damage:** Where the tenant has caused substantial damage to the premises, or uses the premises for a purpose other than that for which they were let, the landlord may seek eviction.
5. **Reconstruction or Demolition:** Where the premises are required for reconstruction, repair, or demolition that cannot be carried out without the premises being vacated.

## Rent Control Acts: General Principles

Rent Control Acts across states generally provide for the fixation of "fair rent" or "standard rent" — a maximum rent that can be charged for a premises. Once fair rent is fixed by the Rent Controller or court, the landlord cannot charge rent exceeding that amount. Tenants are protected from eviction except on the grounds specified in the Act, and eviction can only be ordered by the Rent Controller or competent court. Many states are now enacting Model Tenancy Acts based on the Model Tenancy Act, 2021 proposed by the Central Government, which seeks to balance the interests of landlords and tenants and encourage rental housing.

# Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA)

## Key Provisions of RERA

The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA) was enacted to protect the interests of home buyers, promote transparency, and establish accountability in the real estate sector. RERA applies to both residential and commercial real estate projects. Each state and union territory is required to establish a Real Estate Regulatory Authority.

**Registration of Projects (Section 3):** No promoter shall advertise, market, book, sell, or offer for sale, or invite persons to purchase any plot, apartment, or building in any real estate project without registering the project with the Real Estate Regulatory Authority. Projects on land exceeding 500 square meters or having more than 8 apartments must be registered.

**Builder Obligations (Section 4, 14):** The promoter must provide all details of the project including the sanctioned plan, layout plan, specifications, stage of completion, and expected date of completion. The promoter must deposit 70% of the amount received from allottees in a separate escrow account to be used only for construction purposes. The promoter is liable for structural defects for a period of five years from the date of handing over possession — any defect brought to notice must be rectified within 30 days at the promoter's cost.

**Buyer Protection:** Buyers are entitled to obtain all information relating to the sanctioned plans, layout plans, and specifications. If the promoter fails to give possession on time, the buyer is entitled to a refund with interest, or to continue with the project and receive interest for every month of delay. Buyers can also claim compensation for any loss or damage caused due to incorrect or false information in the advertisement or prospectus.

**Complaint Filing (Section 31):** Any aggrieved person may file a complaint with the Real Estate Regulatory Authority or the adjudicating officer for any violation or contravention of the provisions of the Act or the rules and regulations made thereunder. The Authority must dispose of the complaint within 60 days. An appeal against the order of the Authority lies before the Real Estate Appellate Tribunal, and further appeals may be made to the High Court.

**Penalties:** Non-registration of a project can attract a penalty of up to 10% of the estimated cost of the project. Continued violation may result in imprisonment up to three years. Providing false information can attract a penalty of up to 5% of the estimated cost.
