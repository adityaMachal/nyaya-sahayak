# Labor Laws of India

Covers the Industrial Disputes Act 1947 (ID Act), Shops and Establishment Acts, Payment of Wages Act, Payment of Gratuity Act, Employees' Provident Funds Act, Minimum Wages Act, Maternity Benefit Act, Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act 2013 (POSH), and the New Labour Codes 2020.

# Industrial Disputes Act, 1947

The Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 is the principal legislation governing the investigation and settlement of industrial disputes in India. It provides the machinery and procedure for the investigation and settlement of industrial disputes, and lays down provisions relating to strikes, lockouts, layoffs, retrenchment, and closure. The Act applies to all "industrial establishments" as defined therein.

## Section 2(k): Definition of Industrial Dispute

"Industrial dispute" means any dispute or difference between employers and employers, or between employers and workmen, or between workmen and workmen, which is connected with the employment or non-employment or the terms of employment or with the conditions of labour, of any person. The dispute must relate to an industry and must be raised by a substantial number of workmen or a trade union. An individual dispute can be treated as an industrial dispute if a workman is discharged, dismissed, retrenched, or otherwise terminated and the dispute is referred to by a union or a specified number of workmen.

## Section 9A: Notice of Change in Conditions of Service

No employer who proposes to effect any change in the conditions of service applicable to any workman in respect of any matter specified in the Fourth Schedule shall effect such change without giving to the workmen likely to be affected by such change a notice in the prescribed manner of the nature of the change proposed to be effected. Such notice must be given at least twenty-one days before the change is to be effected. The Fourth Schedule covers matters such as wages, contribution paid or payable by the employer to any provident fund, compensatory and other allowances, hours of work, rest intervals, leave with wages, and rules of discipline.

## Section 25F: Conditions Precedent to Retrenchment of Workmen

No workman employed in any industry who has been in continuous service for not less than one year under an employer shall be retrenched by that employer until: (a) the workman has been given one month's notice in writing indicating the reasons for retrenchment and the period of notice has expired, or the workman has been paid in lieu of such notice, wages for the period of the notice; (b) the workman has been paid, at the time of retrenchment, compensation which shall be equivalent to fifteen days' average pay for every completed year of continuous service or any part thereof in excess of six months; and (c) notice in the prescribed manner is served on the appropriate Government or such authority as may be specified. The retrenchment compensation of fifteen days' average pay per year is a statutory minimum and cannot be reduced by contract.

## Section 25G: Procedure for Retrenchment

Where any workman in an industrial establishment who is a citizen of India is to be retrenched, the employer shall ordinarily retrench the workman who was the last person to be employed in that category, unless for reasons to be recorded the employer retrenches any other workman. This embodies the principle of "last come, first go" or "last in, first out" (LIFO). This principle ensures fairness and prevents arbitrary selection of workmen for retrenchment. The employer must maintain a seniority list of workmen in each category and follow the LIFO principle unless special circumstances justify a departure.

## Section 25N: Conditions for Closure — Permission Required for 100+ Workers

An employer who intends to close down an undertaking of an industrial establishment in which not less than one hundred workmen are employed shall, in the prescribed manner, apply for prior permission to the appropriate Government at least ninety days before the date on which the intended closure is to become effective, stating clearly the reasons for the intended closure. The appropriate Government may grant or refuse permission for closure after conducting an inquiry and giving both parties an opportunity to be heard. If the closure is effected without obtaining permission, such closure shall be deemed to be illegal and the workmen shall be entitled to all benefits as if the closure had not taken place. Similarly, under Section 25N, prior permission is also required for retrenchment of workmen in establishments employing 100 or more workmen.

# Shops and Establishment Acts

## General Provisions (State-Specific)

The Shops and Establishments Acts are state-level legislations that regulate the conditions of work in shops, commercial establishments, restaurants, theatres, and other places of business. While the specific provisions vary from state to state, the following general principles are common across most states.

**Working Hours:** The normal working hours are generally limited to eight or nine hours per day and forty-eight hours per week. No employee shall be required to work for more than the specified number of hours per day, and any work beyond the stipulated hours constitutes overtime.

**Weekly Off:** Every employee is entitled to at least one day of rest per week, which is usually Sunday. In some states, the weekly holiday may be any day of the week, provided it is fixed and regular. No deduction of wages is to be made for the weekly holiday.

**Overtime:** Overtime work must be compensated at double the rate of normal wages in most states. The employer is required to maintain a register of overtime work performed by each employee. There are restrictions on the maximum number of overtime hours an employee can work.

**Leave Entitlements:** Employees are generally entitled to annual leave (earned leave), sick leave, and casual leave. The quantum of leave varies from state to state but is typically around 15-21 days of earned leave per year, 7-12 days of sick leave, and 7-12 days of casual leave. Employees are also entitled to national and festival holidays as notified.

# Payment of Wages Act, 1936

## Key Provisions

The Payment of Wages Act, 1936 ensures the timely and regular payment of wages to employees and prevents unauthorized deductions. It applies to wages payable to an employed person whose wages do not exceed a specified limit.

**Time of Payment (Section 5):** Wages of every person employed in an establishment shall be paid before the expiry of the 7th day after the last day of the wage period for establishments employing fewer than 1,000 workers, and before the expiry of the 10th day for establishments employing 1,000 or more workers. The wage period shall not exceed one month.

**Deductions from Wages (Section 7):** Wages shall be paid without any deductions except those authorized by the Act. Authorized deductions include fines, deductions for absence from duty, deductions for damage or loss caused by the employee's negligence, deductions for services provided (housing, amenities), income tax, court orders, contributions to provident fund, insurance, and cooperative societies. The total deductions from wages shall not exceed 50% of the wages in any wage period. Any unauthorized deduction renders the employer liable for penalties.

# Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972

## Key Provisions

The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 provides for a gratuity payment to employees who have rendered continuous service of not less than five years in an establishment employing ten or more persons. Gratuity is a statutory benefit payable upon termination of employment by way of superannuation, retirement, resignation, death, or disablement.

**Eligibility (Section 4):** Gratuity is payable to an employee on the termination of his employment after he has rendered continuous service for not less than five years. The condition of five years is relaxed in the case of death or disablement. For employees who are not covered by the term "employee" under the Act, contractual gratuity may apply.

**Calculation:** For every completed year of service or part thereof in excess of six months, the employer shall pay gratuity at the rate of fifteen days' wages based on the rate of wages last drawn by the employee. For piece-rated employees, daily wages are computed on the average of the total wages received during the last three months. The wages for calculation purposes are capped such that the maximum amount of gratuity payable shall not exceed twenty lakh rupees (₹20,00,000).

**Forfeiture (Section 4(6)):** The gratuity of an employee whose services have been terminated for any act of wilful omission or negligence causing damage or loss to the employer's property, or for any riotous or disorderly conduct or any other act of violence, or for any act which constitutes an offence involving moral turpitude shall be forfeited wholly or partially.

# Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952

## Key Provisions

The Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952 (EPF Act) provides for the institution of compulsory provident fund, pension fund, and deposit-linked insurance fund for the benefit of employees in factories and other establishments. It applies to every establishment employing 20 or more persons.

**Contribution (Section 6):** Both the employer and the employee contribute to the provident fund at the rate of 12% of the employee's basic wages, dearness allowance, and retaining allowance. The employer's contribution of 12% is divided into 8.33% towards the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS) and 3.67% towards the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF). The employee's entire contribution of 12% goes to the EPF. The employee may voluntarily contribute more than 12%, in which case the excess goes to the Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF).

**Withdrawal Rules:** Complete withdrawal of PF accumulations is permitted upon retirement at 58 years, upon permanent migration out of India, or upon termination of service due to ill health or closure of establishment. Partial withdrawals are permitted for purposes such as purchase or construction of a house (after 5 years of service), marriage of self or children, illness, education of children, and repayment of home loan. Premature withdrawal before 5 years of service attracts TDS at 10% if PAN is provided (30% if PAN is not provided).

**Employees' Pension Scheme:** An employee who has rendered 10 years of eligible service is entitled to a monthly pension upon attaining 58 years of age. Early pension (at a reduced rate) is available from 50 years of age.

# Minimum Wages Act, 1948

## Key Provisions

The Minimum Wages Act, 1948 provides for the fixation of minimum rates of wages in certain employments listed in the Schedule. Both the Central Government and State Governments are empowered to fix minimum wages for employments under their respective jurisdictions. The minimum wages are revised periodically and vary by state, industry, skill level, and zone (metropolitan, urban, semi-urban, rural).

**Fixation and Revision (Section 3-5):** The appropriate government shall fix minimum wages payable to employees employed in scheduled employment. The rates may be fixed by time rate, piece rate, or guaranteed time rate. The minimum wage may consist of a basic rate of wages with a cost-of-living allowance, or an all-inclusive rate. Wages are typically reviewed at intervals not exceeding five years.

**Penalties for Violation (Section 22):** Any employer who pays less than the minimum rate of wages or contravenes any rule or order issued under the Act shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to six months, or with fine which may extend to five hundred rupees, or with both. The employee may also file a claim before the Authority appointed under the Act for recovery of the difference between the minimum wage and the amount actually paid.

# Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (as amended in 2017)

## Key Provisions

The Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 regulates the employment of women in certain establishments for certain periods before and after child-birth and provides for maternity and other benefits. The 2017 amendment significantly enhanced the benefits available to women employees.

**Duration of Maternity Leave (Section 5):** Every woman shall be entitled to, and her employer shall be liable for, the payment of maternity benefit at the rate of the average daily wage for the period of her actual absence — for a maximum period of twenty-six weeks, of which not more than eight weeks shall precede the expected date of delivery. For a woman who has two or more surviving children, the entitlement is twelve weeks (six weeks before and six weeks after delivery). Maternity benefit for adoption and commissioning mothers is twelve weeks from the date the child is handed over to the adoptive or commissioning mother.

**Work from Home Option (Section 5(5)):** The 2017 amendment introduced a provision allowing women to work from home after the period of maternity benefit, if the nature of work assigned to her permits it. The employer and the woman may mutually agree on the duration and terms of the work-from-home arrangement.

**Crèche Facility (Section 11A):** Every establishment having 50 or more employees shall provide a crèche facility within a prescribed distance, either individually or as a common facility. The woman shall be allowed four visits to the crèche daily, which shall include the interval for rest allowed to her. This provision ensures that working mothers have access to childcare facilities at or near the workplace.

**Other Benefits:** Medical bonus if the employer does not provide pre-natal and post-natal care free of charge; nursing breaks of prescribed duration until the child is 15 months old; prohibition of dismissal during maternity leave; and no change to the disadvantage of the woman in any of the conditions of her service during her maternity leave.

# Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 (POSH Act)

## Key Provisions

The POSH Act, 2013 provides protection against sexual harassment of women at the workplace and for the prevention and redressal of complaints of sexual harassment. It is mandatory for every employer of a workplace to constitute an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) where the number of employees is 10 or more.

**Definition of Sexual Harassment (Section 2(n)):** Sexual harassment includes any one or more of the following unwelcome acts or behavior: physical contact and advances; a demand or request for sexual favours; making sexually coloured remarks; showing pornography; any other unwelcome physical, verbal, or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature. It also includes implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment, implied or explicit threat of detrimental treatment, implied or explicit threat about present or future employment status, interference with work or creating an intimidating or hostile work environment, or humiliating treatment likely to affect health or safety.

**Internal Complaints Committee (Section 4):** Every employer of a workplace employing 10 or more workers shall constitute an ICC. The ICC must be presided over by a woman employed at a senior level at the workplace. At least half the members shall be women. The ICC must include an external member from an NGO or association committed to the cause of women, or a person familiar with issues relating to sexual harassment.

**Complaint Procedure (Section 9):** Any aggrieved woman may make a written complaint of sexual harassment at the workplace to the Internal Complaints Committee within a period of three months from the date of the incident. The ICC may extend this period by three months if it is satisfied that the circumstances were such that prevented the woman from filing the complaint within the initial period. The ICC shall complete the inquiry within a period of ninety days from the date of receipt of the complaint.

**Redressal (Section 13):** Upon completion of inquiry, if the ICC concludes that the allegation is proved, it shall recommend to the employer to take action for sexual harassment as a misconduct in accordance with service rules, or if no service rules exist, recommend actions including written apology, warning, reprimand, withholding of promotion or pay rise, termination of employment, or counselling. The ICC may also recommend deduction from salary or payment of compensation to the aggrieved woman.

**Timelines and Penalties:** The employer must act on the ICC's recommendations within 60 days. If the employer fails to constitute an ICC or violates other provisions, the employer shall be punishable with fine which may extend to ₹50,000. A repeated offence may result in higher penalties and cancellation of the establishment's license.

# New Labour Codes, 2020 (Overview)

## Overview of the Four Labour Codes

The Government of India has consolidated 29 existing central labor laws into four comprehensive Labour Codes passed in 2019-2020. These Codes aim to simplify and rationalize labor regulations, improve ease of doing business, and extend social security benefits to a wider workforce including gig and platform workers.

**Code on Wages, 2019:** This Code subsumes the Payment of Wages Act 1936, the Minimum Wages Act 1948, the Payment of Bonus Act 1965, and the Equal Remuneration Act 1976. It provides for a universal minimum wage applicable to all employees across organized and unorganized sectors. A national floor wage will be set by the Central Government, below which no state can fix a minimum wage. Wages must be paid within the 7th of the following month. The definition of "wages" has been standardized to mean at least 50% of the total remuneration, ensuring that basic pay is not artificially suppressed.

**Industrial Relations Code, 2020:** This Code subsumes the Industrial Disputes Act 1947, the Trade Unions Act 1926, and the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946. Key changes include: the threshold for requiring government permission for layoff, retrenchment, and closure has been raised from 100 to 300 workers; the concept of a negotiating union or negotiating council for collective bargaining has been introduced; a 14-day notice is required before going on strike (extended from the previous requirement applicable only to public utility services); and the establishment of Industrial Tribunals for adjudication of disputes.

**Social Security Code, 2020:** This Code subsumes the EPF Act 1952, the ESI Act 1948, the Maternity Benefit Act 1961, the Payment of Gratuity Act 1972, the Employees' Compensation Act 1923, the Building and Other Construction Workers' Act 1996, and others. It extends social security benefits to gig workers, platform workers, and unorganized sector workers through a Social Security Fund. Aggregators (such as ride-hailing apps and delivery platforms) shall contribute 1-2% of their annual turnover towards this fund. The Code also provides for an electronic system of registration and compliance.

**Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (OSH Code):** This Code subsumes 13 existing laws including the Factories Act 1948, the Mines Act 1952, the Contract Labour Act 1970, and the Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act 1979. It provides for a single registration for an establishment, simplified compliance, and uniform provisions for working conditions. Key provisions include: an annual health check-up for workers above a prescribed age; an appointment letter for every employee; improved conditions for inter-state migrant workers including a travel allowance; and working hours not exceeding 8 hours per day with overtime compensation at twice the normal rate. Women are permitted to work in all establishments including night shifts, subject to safety conditions.

**Implementation Status:** As of the latest updates, the four Labour Codes have been passed by Parliament but the rules thereunder are being framed by both Central and State Governments. The codes will come into effect once the rules are notified. Several states have published draft rules, and the Codes are expected to be implemented in a phased manner.
