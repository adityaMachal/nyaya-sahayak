# Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (Amended 2019)

An Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to motor vehicles. The Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (Act No. 59 of 1988) was significantly amended by the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019 (Act No. 32 of 2019), which introduced stricter penalties for traffic violations, provisions for electronic enforcement, Good Samaritan protections, and enhanced compensation for road accident victims. Source: [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/).

---

## Section 3: Necessity for Driving Licence

No person shall drive a motor vehicle in any public place unless he holds an effective driving licence issued to him authorizing him to drive the vehicle. No person under the age of eighteen years shall drive a motor vehicle in any public place. A person may drive a motor vehicle of the class of transport vehicle if he holds a valid driving licence for that class and has undergone the requisite training. Any person who drives a motor vehicle in contravention of this section shall be punishable as provided under the relevant penalty sections of this Act.

## Section 4: Age Limit in Connection with Driving of Motor Vehicles

No person under the age of eighteen years shall drive a motor vehicle in any public place. However, a person of sixteen years of age or above may drive a motor vehicle with engine capacity not exceeding 50cc in a public place, subject to conditions prescribed by the State Government. No person under the age of twenty years shall drive a transport vehicle in any public place. For obtaining a licence to drive a transport vehicle, the applicant must be at least twenty years of age and must hold a licence to drive a light motor vehicle for at least one year. A learner's licence holder may drive a vehicle only when accompanied by a licensed instructor or a person holding a valid driving licence for that class of vehicle.

---

## Section 15: Learner's Licence

Any person who is not disqualified under Section 4 may apply to the licensing authority having jurisdiction in the area in which the applicant resides or carries on business for the issue to him of a learner's licence. A learner's licence shall be valid for a period of six months from the date of issue. The holder of a learner's licence shall drive a vehicle only when accompanied by the instructor or by a person who holds a permanent driving licence for the type of vehicle being driven. The learner shall display a distinguishing sign (red letter "L" on a white background) on the vehicle. A learner's licence may be issued in electronic form and may carry a digital photograph and digital signature.

## Section 112: Speed Limits

The State Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, fix the maximum speed at which motor vehicles may be driven in any area or on any specified road. The Central Government may also prescribe the maximum speed for different classes of motor vehicles on national highways. If no specific speed limit is prescribed, the maximum speed for cars is generally 70 km/h in cities and 120 km/h on expressways as per Motor Vehicle Rules. Whoever drives a motor vehicle in excess of the speed limit prescribed for the area or the class of vehicle shall be punishable with a fine which may extend to four thousand rupees (₹4,000) for the first offence and up to ten thousand rupees (₹10,000) for a subsequent offence.

---

## Section 128: Safety Measures — Seat Belts

Every motor vehicle of such class or description as may be prescribed by the Central Government shall be equipped with such safety devices including seat belts as may be prescribed. The driver and all passengers of a motor vehicle shall wear seat belts while the vehicle is in motion. Failure to wear a seat belt by the driver or passengers is punishable with a fine of one thousand rupees (₹1,000). The Central Government may prescribe standards for seat belts and other safety devices to be fitted in motor vehicles.

## Section 129: Safety Measures — Helmets

Every person driving or riding on a two-wheeled motorcycle shall wear protective headgear (helmet) conforming to the standards of the Bureau of Indian Standards. The State Government may, by rules, require that every person driving or riding on a motorcycle in any public place shall wear a protective headgear conforming to such standards. Failure to wear a helmet while driving or riding a two-wheeler is punishable with a fine of one thousand rupees (₹1,000) and disqualification to hold licence for a period of three months. The protective headgear must conform to the ISI standard and shall be of such type as may be specified by the Central Government.

## Section 177: General Offences and Penalties

Whoever contravenes any provision of this Act or of any rule, regulation, or notification made thereunder for which no separate penalty is provided shall be punishable with a fine of five hundred rupees (₹500) for a first offence and one thousand five hundred rupees (₹1,500) for a second or subsequent offence. If the offence is committed by a juvenile, the guardian or owner of the vehicle shall be deemed to be guilty of the contravention and shall be punished accordingly, and the registration of the motor vehicle shall be cancelled. The Act also provides for compounding of certain offences by designated officers.

---

## Section 183: Driving at Excessive Speed — Penalty

Whoever drives a motor vehicle in contravention of the speed limits prescribed under this Act or the rules made thereunder shall be punishable with a fine of up to four thousand rupees (₹4,000) for the first offence and up to ten thousand rupees (₹10,000) for any second or subsequent offence. For light motor vehicles, the speed limits are as prescribed by the State or Central Government. For heavy vehicles and transport vehicles, exceeding speed limits may also attract suspension of the driving licence. The offence may be detected and enforced through electronic enforcement devices, including speed cameras and automatic speed detection systems.

---

## Section 184: Driving Dangerously

Whoever drives a motor vehicle at a speed or in a manner which is dangerous to the public, having regard to all the circumstances of the case, including the nature, condition, and use of the place and the amount of traffic which actually is or might reasonably be expected to be in the place, shall be punishable for the first offence with imprisonment for a term which may extend to six months or with fine up to five thousand rupees (₹5,000), or with both. For a second or subsequent offence, the punishment is imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years or with fine up to ten thousand rupees (₹10,000), or with both. The court may also order the suspension of the driving licence of the offender.

---

## Section 185: Driving by a Drunken Person or by a Person Under the Influence of Drugs

Whoever, while driving or attempting to drive a motor vehicle, has in his blood alcohol exceeding 30 mg per 100 ml of blood detected in a test by a breath analyser, shall be punishable for the first offence with imprisonment for a term which may extend to six months or with fine up to ten thousand rupees (₹10,000), or with both. For a second or subsequent offence committed within three years of the previous offence, the punishment is imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years or with fine up to fifteen thousand rupees (₹15,000), or with both. The driving licence of the person convicted shall be suspended for at least six months for the first offence. Refusal to submit to a breath test or blood test when required by a police officer constitutes a separate offence punishable under this section.

---

## Section 186: Driving Without Licence

Whoever drives a motor vehicle in any public place without holding a valid driving licence, or in contravention of the conditions of the licence, shall be punishable with a fine of five thousand rupees (₹5,000). If a person drives a motor vehicle without a licence and causes an accident resulting in injury or death, the penalty shall be enhanced. The owner of the vehicle who permits any person to drive the vehicle without a valid driving licence shall also be liable to punishment with imprisonment which may extend to three months or with fine of up to twenty-five thousand rupees (₹25,000), or with both.

---

## Section 189: Racing and Speed Trials

Whoever permits or takes part in a race or trial of speed of any kind between motor vehicles in any public place shall be punishable with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year or with fine up to five thousand rupees (₹5,000), or with both. For a subsequent offence, the punishment is imprisonment which may extend to two years or fine up to ten thousand rupees (₹10,000), or with both. The organizer of such race or speed trial on a public road shall be liable for a higher penalty, and the vehicles involved may be impounded. Any person who abets or encourages racing on highways shall also be punishable under this section.

---

## Section 194: Driving Without Insurance

Whoever drives a motor vehicle or causes or allows a motor vehicle to be driven in contravention of the requirement of valid insurance policy (Section 146) shall be punishable with imprisonment which may extend to three months or with fine of two thousand rupees (₹2,000), or with both. For a second or subsequent offence, the fine increases. The 2019 Amendment enhanced the penalties significantly. A motor vehicle found without insurance may be impounded by the police, and the registration of the vehicle may be suspended until valid insurance is produced. Third-party insurance is mandatory for all motor vehicles plying on public roads.

---

## Section 140: Liability to Pay Compensation in Certain Cases on the Principle of No Fault

Where death or permanent disablement of any person has resulted from an accident arising out of the use of a motor vehicle, the owner of the vehicle or the authorized insurer shall be liable to pay compensation to the legal representatives of the deceased or to the injured person, as the case may be, whether or not there has been any wrongful act, neglect, or default of the owner or any other person. The amount of compensation for death in such no-fault cases is fixed at a minimum of two lakh rupees (₹2,00,000) under the 2019 Amendment. For permanent disablement, the minimum compensation shall be fifty thousand rupees (₹50,000). This compensation is payable irrespective of any other claim or compensation that the claimant may pursue under the fault-based liability provisions.

## Section 141: Provisions as to Other Right to Claim Compensation for Death or Permanent Disablement

The right to claim compensation under Section 140 shall be in addition to any other right to claim compensation in respect thereof under any other provision of this Act or any other law for the time being in force. The claimant is not required to plead or establish that the death or permanent disablement was due to any wrongful act, neglect, or default of the owner or any person. Any amount paid or payable under Section 140 shall be deducted from the amount of any compensation awarded under Section 166 (fault-based compensation).

## Section 142: Permanent Disablement

For the purposes of this Act, permanent disablement of a person shall be deemed to have resulted from an accident if such person has suffered, as a result of the accident, the loss of use of any member or joint, the loss of any faculty, or the permanent disfigurement of the head or face. The injury shall be of such nature as is specified in Schedule I of the Act. The extent of permanent disablement is determined by medical examination and assessment.

## Section 143: Provisions Regarding Insurance

The owner of the motor vehicle or the authorized insurer liable to pay compensation shall be liable to pay that compensation as provided in Section 140, whether or not the vehicle was insured at the time of the accident. Where the vehicle was not insured, the compensation shall be recovered from the owner. The insurer shall have the right to recover any compensation paid from the owner if the vehicle was being used in contravention of the terms of the insurance policy.

## Section 144: Overriding Effect

The provisions of Section 140 shall have effect notwithstanding anything contained in any other provision of this Act or of any other law for the time being in force. The no-fault liability principle ensures that accident victims receive at least the minimum statutory compensation without the burden of proving fault or negligence. This is a social security measure designed to provide immediate relief to road accident victims.

## Section 161: Motor Accidents Fund

The Central Government shall constitute a fund to be called the Motor Accidents Fund for the purpose of providing compulsory insurance cover to all road users in India, treatment of persons injured in road accidents in accordance with the scheme framed by the Central Government, compensation to representatives of a person who dies in a hit-and-run motor accident, and compensation to persons grievously hurt in a hit-and-run accident. The Fund shall be credited with amounts from the Central Government, surcharge on motor insurance premiums, and grants or loans from any other source.

## Section 162: Scheme for Interim Relief to Claimants

The Central Government shall make a scheme for the purpose of providing interim relief to claimants praying for compensation under Section 166 or Section 163. The interim relief shall be provided within a period of sixty days of filing of the claim application. The scheme shall specify the categories of claimants, amount of interim relief, and the procedure for disbursement. The aim is to ensure that accident victims receive immediate financial support while their full claims are being adjudicated.

## Section 163: Motor Accident Claims Tribunal and Hit-and-Run Compensation

For the purpose of adjudicating upon claims for compensation in respect of accidents involving death or bodily injury arising out of the use of motor vehicles, the State Government shall constitute one or more Motor Accident Claims Tribunals (MACT) for such area as may be specified. The Claims Tribunal shall consist of a person who is or has been a Judge of a High Court or a District Judge or is qualified to be appointed as a High Court Judge. For hit-and-run cases (where the identity of the vehicle causing the accident is unknown or the driver flees), compensation is payable from the Motor Accidents Fund — two lakh rupees (₹2,00,000) in case of death and fifty thousand rupees (₹50,000) in case of grievous hurt, as enhanced by the 2019 Amendment.

## Section 164: Payment of Compensation in Case of Death or Grievous Hurt

Where death or grievous hurt has been caused to a person as a result of a motor vehicle accident, the Claims Tribunal shall arrange for the payment of compensation as specified in a structured formula provided in the Second Schedule. The compensation shall be based on the income of the victim, the age of the victim, and a multiplier prescribed by law. The minimum compensation for death shall not be less than five lakh rupees (₹5,00,000). For cases involving fatal accidents, the Claims Tribunal shall make an award within six months from the date of filing. The compensation shall include funeral expenses, loss of consortium, and loss of estate, in addition to the loss of future earnings.

## Good Samaritan Provisions (Section 134A and Guidelines)

The 2019 Amendment provides statutory protection to Good Samaritans who come forward to help accident victims. A Good Samaritan is defined as a person who, in good faith, voluntarily and without expectation of any reward or compensation, comes forward to render emergency medical or non-medical care or assistance at the scene of an accident or to transport the victim to the nearest hospital. No Good Samaritan shall be liable for any civil or criminal action for any injury to or death of the victim of an accident, caused due to their negligence in acting or failing to act in good faith while rendering emergency care. Good Samaritans shall not be compelled to disclose their personal details to the police or at the hospital, and shall not be detained or harassed by the police or hospital authorities. A Good Samaritan who has witnessed an accident and has voluntarily reported the matter may be examined on a single occasion by the investigating officer, but the examination shall be conducted at a time and place of the Good Samaritan's convenience.
