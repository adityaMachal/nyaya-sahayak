# Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (as amended by Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019)

**Act No. 59 of 1988** | **Amendment Act No. 32 of 2019**
Source: [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in)

An Act to consolidate and amend the law relating to motor vehicles. The Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019, received Presidential assent on 9th August, 2019, and introduced significantly enhanced penalties, provisions for road safety, electronic enforcement, Good Samaritan protections, and regulation of aggregators.

---

## Section 3: Necessity for Driving Licence

No person shall drive a motor vehicle in any public place unless he holds an effective driving licence issued to him authorising him to drive the vehicle. No person shall so drive a transport vehicle other than a motor cab or motorcycle hired for his own use or rented under any scheme made under Section 75 unless his driving licence specifically entitles him to do so. Any person who drives a motor vehicle in contravention of this section shall be punishable as provided under Section 181 of the Act. The licence must be carried by the driver at all times while driving and must be produced on demand by any police officer or authority empowered under this Act.

## Section 4: Age Limit in Connection with Driving of Motor Vehicles

No person under the age of **eighteen years** shall drive a motor vehicle in any public place. However, a person who has completed the age of **sixteen years** may drive a motor vehicle of engine capacity not exceeding 50cc (such as a gearless scooter/moped) in a public place, subject to the condition that such person has obtained a valid driving licence. No person under the age of **twenty years** shall drive a transport vehicle in any public place. The age limits are designed to ensure that drivers possess the necessary maturity and physical capability to handle different categories of vehicles safely.

---

## Section 15: Learner's Licence

Any person who is not disqualified under Section 4 for driving a motor vehicle and who does not already hold a driving licence may apply for a learner's licence. The licensing authority shall, if satisfied that the applicant is eligible, issue a learner's licence within thirty days authorising the holder to drive a motor vehicle as a learner. A learner's licence shall be valid for a period of **six months** from the date of issue.

A person holding a learner's licence may drive a motor vehicle in a public place provided: (a) the vehicle is fitted with a sign marked "L" displayed prominently on the front and rear of the vehicle; (b) the holder, when driving a four-wheeled vehicle, is accompanied by a person holding a valid driving licence for that type of vehicle and sitting beside the holder; and (c) the holder shall not carry any person on the pillion of a two-wheeled vehicle. The holder of a learner's licence may apply for a driving licence after a period of thirty days from the date of issue of the learner's licence, subject to passing the driving test.

---

## Section 66: Necessity for Permits for Transport Vehicles

No owner of a motor vehicle shall use or permit the use of the vehicle as a transport vehicle in any public place, whether or not such vehicle is actually carrying any passengers or goods, save in accordance with the conditions of a permit granted or countersigned by a Regional or State Transport Authority authorising the use of the vehicle in that place. The types of permits include: (a) **Stage carriage permit** — for carrying passengers for hire along a defined route; (b) **Contract carriage permit** — for carrying passengers under a contract for the hire of the vehicle as a whole; (c) **Goods carriage permit** — for transporting goods; (d) **All India Tourist permit** — for tourist vehicles; and (e) **National permit** — for goods carriages to operate across state boundaries.

The 2019 Amendment simplified the permit regime by introducing provisions for **national transport policy** and enabling the Central Government to issue guidelines for grant of permits. It also introduced the concept of the **National Transportation Policy** to plan and develop a framework for the grant of permits ensuring adequate transport services.

## Section 112: Speed Limits

The State Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, fix the maximum speed at which motor vehicles may be driven in any area or on any road. The Central Government may also prescribe the maximum speed for different classes of vehicles. As per the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, the speed limits generally prescribed are: **cars and SUVs — 70 km/h in cities, 100-120 km/h on expressways**; **motorcycles — 60 km/h in cities**; **buses — 60-80 km/h depending on category**; **goods vehicles — 60-80 km/h depending on laden/unladen state**. Any person who drives a motor vehicle in excess of the speed limit shall be liable for punishment under Section 183.

The State Government or the authority empowered may install speed cameras and electronic enforcement devices to detect speed limit violations. Evidence gathered through such electronic means is admissible in proceedings under this Act. Speed limits may vary for different stretches of road, different times of day, and different types of vehicles, and shall be clearly displayed through signage at relevant locations.

---

## Section 113: Limits on Weight and Restrictions on Use

No person shall drive or cause or allow to be driven in any public place any motor vehicle or trailer the laden weight of which exceeds the gross vehicle weight specified in the certificate of registration of the vehicle. No person shall drive or cause to be driven any motor vehicle towing a trailer or any other motor vehicle if the laden weight of the trailer or the other vehicle exceeds the maximum limit prescribed. Overloading of goods vehicles beyond the permissible weight is an offence punishable under Section 194 of the Act.

The 2019 Amendment enhanced penalties for overloading: a fine of **₹20,000 plus ₹2,000 per extra tonne** is imposed for overloading. If the vehicle is a transport vehicle, the permit may also be suspended or cancelled. Overloading is a major cause of road accidents and infrastructure damage, and the enhanced penalties aim to deter this practice and protect road safety.

---

## Section 128: Safety Measures — Seat Belts

Every motor vehicle fitted with a seat belt shall be driven with the seat belt fastened by the driver and every person sitting in the front seat. The Central Government may, by rules, require that seat belts be worn by passengers in rear seats as well. Failure to wear seat belts is punishable with a fine of **₹1,000** under the 2019 Amendment (previously ₹100). The Rules may prescribe specifications and standards for seat belts to be fitted in motor vehicles and the manner in which such seat belts are to be used.

## Section 129: Safety Measures — Helmets

Every person driving or riding on a two-wheeled motorcycle shall wear a protective headgear (helmet) conforming to the standards of the Bureau of Indian Standards. The 2019 Amendment raised the penalty for not wearing a helmet to **₹1,000** and a disqualification of licence for **three months**. The State Government may exempt any class of persons or any area from the operation of this section. The helmet must meet ISI (BIS) standards as prescribed under the Central Motor Vehicle Rules, 1989. Children riding on two-wheelers as pillion passengers are also required to wear helmets of appropriate size.

---

## Section 177: General Provision for Punishment of Offences

Whoever contravenes any provision of this Act or of any rule, regulation, or notification made thereunder shall, if no penalty is provided for the offence, be punishable for the first offence with a fine which may extend to **₹500** (enhanced to **₹5,000** by the 2019 Amendment) and for any second or subsequent offence with a fine which may extend to **₹10,000** (enhanced by the 2019 Amendment from ₹1,500). This is the residuary or general penalty provision that applies to any contravention not specifically dealt with elsewhere in the Act. The enhanced penalties under the 2019 Amendment reflect the Government's intent to significantly deter traffic violations.

---

## Section 183: Driving at Excessive Speed — Penalty

Whoever drives a motor vehicle in contravention of the speed limit referred to in Section 112 shall be punishable with a fine of **₹1,000 for a first offence** and **₹2,000 for a second or subsequent offence** for light motor vehicles (under the pre-2019 regime). The 2019 Amendment enhanced these penalties significantly: for light motor vehicles, the fine is **₹1,000 to ₹2,000 for first offence** and **₹2,000 to ₹4,000 for second or subsequent offence**. For medium and heavy vehicles, the fine ranges from **₹2,000 to ₹5,000**.

Additionally, the driving licence of the offender may be impounded for a period ranging from three months to six months for repeated offences. Speed violations detected through electronic enforcement devices (speed cameras, radar guns) shall also attract the same penalties. The licensing authority may conduct a remedial driving course requirement before restoring the licence.

---

## Section 184: Driving Dangerously

Whoever drives a motor vehicle at a speed or in a manner which is dangerous to the public, having regard to all the circumstances of the case, including the nature, condition, and use of the place where the vehicle is driven, and the amount of traffic which is actually at the time or might reasonably be expected to be in the place, shall be punishable for the first offence with **imprisonment for a term which may extend to six months, or with fine which may extend to ₹1,000, or with both**. For any second or subsequent offence, the punishment is **imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years, or with fine which may extend to ₹2,000, or with both**.

The 2019 Amendment enhanced these penalties: for a first offence, **imprisonment up to six months to one year and/or fine of ₹1,000 to ₹5,000**. For a second or subsequent offence committed within three years, the imprisonment may extend to **two years** and the fine to **₹10,000**. The court may also order suspension or revocation of the driving licence. Dangerous driving causing death of any person is punishable under Section 304A of the Indian Penal Code with imprisonment up to two years.

---

## Section 185: Driving by a Drunken Person or by a Person Under the Influence of Drugs

Whoever, while driving or attempting to drive a motor vehicle, has in his blood alcohol exceeding **30 mg per 100 ml of blood** as detected by a breath analyser shall be punishable for the **first offence** with **imprisonment for a term which may extend to six months and/or with fine up to ₹10,000**. For a **second or subsequent offence** committed within three years of the previous offence, the punishment is **imprisonment for a term which may extend to two years and/or with fine of ₹15,000**.

The 2019 Amendment significantly enhanced drunk driving penalties from the earlier ₹2,000 fine. A police officer in uniform may require any person driving or attempting to drive a motor vehicle to provide a specimen of breath for a breath analysis test, and refusal to undergo such test shall be deemed an offence equivalent to a positive test result. The driving licence of the offender shall be suspended for a period of at least three months for first offence and at least twelve months for subsequent offences. The offender may also be required to attend a course on the effects of alcohol and drugs on driving.

---

## Section 186: Driving Without Licence

Whoever drives a motor vehicle in any public place without holding a valid driving licence shall be punishable with a fine which may extend to **₹5,000** (enhanced from ₹500 by the 2019 Amendment). If a person is found driving a vehicle of a class for which he does not hold a licence, the same penalty applies. Where a person whose driving licence has been suspended or revoked drives a motor vehicle in a public place, the person shall be punishable with the same penalty. The vehicle may be impounded and the person may be required to obtain a valid licence before the vehicle is released. If the person causes any accident while driving without a licence, the penalties are aggravated and the person may face criminal prosecution.

---

## Section 189: Racing and Trials of Speed

Whoever without the written consent of the State Government permits, or takes part in, a race or trial of speed of any kind between motor vehicles in any public place, shall be punishable with **imprisonment for a term which may extend to one month, or with fine which may extend to ₹500, or with both** (pre-2019). The 2019 Amendment enhanced this to **imprisonment up to one year and/or fine up to ₹5,000** for organising or participating in illegal racing or speed trials on public roads. For any subsequent offence, the imprisonment may extend to **two years** and the fine to **₹10,000**.

The vehicle used in such racing may be seized and impounded. The licensing authority may suspend or cancel the driving licence of any person convicted under this section. This provision is aimed at curbing dangerous illegal street racing and drag racing on public roads, which poses severe risks to other road users and pedestrians.

---

## Section 194: Driving Uninsured Vehicle

Whoever drives or allows a motor vehicle to be driven in a public place without a valid policy of insurance as required under Chapter XI (Insurance of Motor Vehicles Against Third Party Risks) shall be punishable with **imprisonment which may extend to three months, or with a fine of ₹2,000, or with both** (pre-2019 penalties). The 2019 Amendment enhanced the penalty to **a fine of ₹2,000** and/or imprisonment. Third-party motor vehicle insurance is compulsory for all vehicles plying on public roads.

The insurance must cover liability to third parties for death, bodily injury, or damage to property arising out of the use of the vehicle. The vehicle may be impounded until a valid insurance policy is produced. Driving without insurance is not only a criminal offence but also exposes the vehicle owner to unlimited personal liability for damages in case of an accident. The Motor Vehicles Act mandates that every motor vehicle must have at least a third-party insurance policy.

---

## Section 196: Using Vehicle Without Permit

Whoever drives or causes or allows a transport vehicle to be used without a valid permit as required under Section 66 shall be punishable with a fine which may extend to **₹10,000** (enhanced from ₹5,000 by the 2019 Amendment). For a second or subsequent offence, the fine may extend to **₹10,000** along with the possibility of the vehicle being seized. The permit conditions include the route, the area of operation, the number of passengers or weight of goods that may be carried, and other conditions as the transport authority may specify.

Operating without a permit undermines the regulatory framework for public transport, compromises passenger safety, and amounts to unfair competition with licensed operators. Aggregator-operated vehicles are also required to comply with permit requirements under the 2019 amendments.

---

## Section 140: Liability to Pay Compensation in Certain Cases on the Principle of No Fault

Where death or permanent disablement of any person has resulted from an accident arising out of the use of a motor vehicle, the owner of the vehicle or the authorised insurer shall be liable to pay compensation to the victim or their legal representatives on the principle of no fault. This means that the claimant does not need to prove negligence or any wrongful act on the part of the vehicle owner or driver. The amount of no-fault compensation fixed is **₹50,000 in case of death** and **₹25,000 in case of permanent disablement** (these amounts were enhanced by the 2019 Amendment to ₹2,00,000 for death and ₹50,000 for grievous injury in certain proposals).

## Section 141: Provisions as to Other Right to Claim Compensation for Death or Permanent Disablement

The right to claim compensation under Section 140 shall be in addition to any other right to claim compensation in respect thereof under any other provision of this Act or of any other law for the time being in force. A claim for compensation under Section 140 shall be disposed of as expeditiously as possible and where compensation is claimed in respect of death or permanent disablement under Section 140 and also under any other provision of this Act, the amount paid under Section 140 shall be adjusted against the total compensation awarded.

## Section 142: Permanent Disablement Defined

For the purposes of this Chapter, "permanent disablement" of a person shall be deemed to have resulted from an accident of the nature referred to in Section 140 if such person has suffered loss of use of any part of the body as specified, including loss of a hand, foot, or eye, or severe facial disfigurement, or total and permanent loss of hearing, or absolute deafness. The definition is aligned with the schedule to the Workmen's Compensation Act to ensure consistency in interpretation.

## Section 143: Accident Arising Out of Use of Motor Vehicle

For the purposes of this Chapter, an accident shall be deemed to arise out of the use of a motor vehicle if it arises in the course of the driving of the vehicle, or in the course of a person mounting or alighting from the vehicle, or relating to the handling of goods in connection with the vehicle. This broad definition ensures that victims of motor vehicle accidents receive compensation regardless of the specific circumstances in which the accident occurred.

## Section 144: Overriding Effect

The provisions of this Chapter shall have overriding effect notwithstanding anything contained in any other provision of this Act or of any other law for the time being in force. This ensures that the no-fault liability regime cannot be circumvented by relying on other legal provisions that might limit or exclude liability.

## Section 161: Special Provisions as to Compensation in Case of Hit-and-Run Motor Accidents

Where a person is a victim of a hit-and-run motor accident (where the identity of the vehicle involved or the owner/driver thereof is not ascertainable), the Central Government shall pay compensation to the victim or their legal representatives. The compensation amount shall be as prescribed by the Central Government: under the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act, 2019, this is **₹2,00,000 in case of death** (enhanced from ₹25,000) and **₹50,000 in case of grievous hurt** (enhanced from ₹12,500). The scheme for payment of such compensation is funded through the **Motor Vehicle Accident Fund** established under Section 164B of the Act.

## Section 162: Refund in Certain Cases

The Central Government or the State Government or the insurer may recover the amount paid as compensation under Section 161 from the owner or driver of the vehicle if the identity of the vehicle and owner/driver is subsequently ascertained. Proceedings for recovery may be initiated as an arrear of land revenue. This provision ensures that the hit-and-run compensation fund is replenished where the responsible party is later identified.

## Section 163: Motor Accidents Claims Tribunal

The State Government shall, by notification, constitute one or more Motor Accident Claims Tribunals (MACT) for such area as may be specified in the notification, for the purpose of adjudicating upon claims for compensation in respect of accidents involving the death of or bodily injury to persons arising out of the use of motor vehicles. A Claims Tribunal shall consist of such number of members as the State Government thinks fit, and each member shall be a person who is or has been a Judge of a High Court or is or has been a District Judge. The Tribunal shall follow summary procedure and has the powers of a civil court.

## Section 164: Award of the Claims Tribunal

The Claims Tribunal shall make an award determining the amount of compensation which appears to it to be just. The Tribunal considers factors such as the age and income of the victim, the nature and extent of injuries, the loss of earning capacity, medical expenses incurred, pain and suffering, and the loss of consortium or companionship to family members. The Tribunal shall arrange to deliver copies of the award to the parties concerned expeditiously. Awards of the Claims Tribunal may be appealed to the High Court within ninety days. The 2019 Amendment introduced a structured formula based on the **multiplier method** for computing compensation in death cases, based on the annual income of the deceased and the age at the time of death.

## Section 199A: Licensing of Aggregators

The 2019 Amendment introduced Section 199A to regulate ride-hailing aggregators such as Ola and Uber. An **"aggregator"** is defined as a digital intermediary or marketplace for a passenger to connect with a driver for the purpose of transportation. Every aggregator shall obtain a licence from the State Government or such authority as the State Government may appoint, which shall be valid for a period of five years and may be renewed.

The aggregator must comply with the following conditions: (a) all vehicles attached to the aggregator must hold valid permits and insurance; (b) all drivers must hold valid driving licences; (c) the aggregator must provide information on individual trips to the State Government when required; (d) the aggregator must comply with the Information Technology Act, 2000 and rules made thereunder; (e) the aggregator must ensure the safety of passengers, especially women; and (f) the aggregator must establish a mechanism for redressal of passenger grievances. The aggregator is liable for penalties if it allows unlicensed drivers or uninsured/unpermitted vehicles to operate on its platform. The State Government may suspend or cancel the licence of an aggregator for non-compliance.

## Good Samaritan Provisions (Section 134A and Guidelines)

The 2019 Amendment introduced formal **Good Samaritan protections** to encourage bystanders and passersby to help road accident victims without fear of legal harassment. Section 134A provides that a Good Samaritan — any person who renders emergency medical or non-medical care or assistance at the scene of an accident or transports the victim to the nearest hospital — shall not be liable for any civil or criminal action for any injury to or death of the victim of the accident.

Key protections include: (a) No Good Samaritan shall be compelled to disclose their personal details such as name, phone number, or address to the police or at the hospital; (b) No Good Samaritan shall be required to become a witness in any civil or criminal case related to the accident; (c) Hospitals shall not delay treatment of accident victims brought by Good Samaritans on grounds of police formalities or payment; (d) Investigation and examination of the Good Samaritan shall be conducted at a time and place of their convenience. These protections are backed by the **Supreme Court's guidelines in Savelife Foundation vs. Union of India (2016)**, which directed States to implement Good Samaritan policies. The Government has also issued detailed **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)** for protection of Good Samaritans.
