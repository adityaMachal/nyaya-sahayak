# Criminal Procedure and Police Rules

## Section 173 BNSS: Filing an FIR and Police Refusal
Under Section 173 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) [formerly Section 154 CrPC], every information relating to the commission of a cognizable offence must be recorded by the officer in charge of a police station. This is known as a First Information Report (FIR).
If a police officer refuses to record the information, the aggrieved person has the right to send the substance of such information, in writing and by post, to the Superintendent of Police (SP) or the Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP). If the SP/DCP is satisfied that the information discloses a cognizable offence, they shall either investigate the case themselves or direct a subordinate police officer to investigate it.

## Section 175 BNSS: Complaint to Magistrate
If both the police station and the Superintendent of Police refuse to register the FIR, the victim can approach a Judicial Magistrate under Section 175(3) of the BNSS [formerly Section 156(3) CrPC]. The Magistrate has the power to direct the police to register an FIR and begin the investigation. The applicant must file a formal application through a lawyer before the Magistrate.

## Zero FIR
A "Zero FIR" can be filed at any police station, regardless of whether the offence occurred within its jurisdiction. Once a Zero FIR is registered, it is given the serial number "0" and is subsequently transferred to the police station that holds the actual jurisdiction to investigate the crime. This ensures that crucial evidence is not lost and the victim does not have to run from pillar to post in an emergency.

## E-FIR for Specific Crimes
Under the BNSS, citizens can file an e-FIR (electronic FIR) for crimes where the accused is unknown, such as theft of property or mobile phones, and for certain minor offences. The e-FIR must be signed by the complainant within 3 days for it to be formally taken on record.

## Arrest Guidelines (DK Basu Guidelines)
When making an arrest, police officers must wear clear identification. They must prepare an arrest memo at the time of arrest, which must be attested by at least one witness (a family member or respectable member of the locality) and countersigned by the person arrested. The arrested person has the right to have a friend or relative informed about the arrest immediately.
